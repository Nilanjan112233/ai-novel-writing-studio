# Working with the Writing Coach

The Workspace Companion changes emphasis when the author enters Drafting or
Editorial. It remains the same companion—with the same memory, dismiss,
snooze, cooldown, and recommendation identity—but its first action comes from
the live Writing Desk.

## What it helps with

- explains the exact planning check blocking a final draft;
- offers a direct action to prepare or open the relevant scene;
- points out duplicated scene plans before prose is generated;
- identifies a draft waiting for review or revision;
- links to Editorial when a structured pass is useful;
- recommends approval only after the existing validation gates pass;
- continues to the next planned scene after approval.

The companion does not generate or approve prose by itself. It explains why an
action is useful and waits for the author.

## Keeping it quiet

Recommendations have stable identities. Dismissed, completed, and snoozed
items are remembered, and cooldowns prevent the same message from appearing
continuously. Reduced-motion preferences disable nonessential movement while
preserving status and focus behavior.

## Asking questions

The Project Assistant answers application questions from Application Knowledge
and writing-state questions from the Writing Desk before searching story
content. Useful questions include:

- Why is writing blocked?
- What should I revise next?
- What did the AI change?
- Which version is safe to restore?
- Where do I review a canon proposal?
- What happens if I approve this scene?

Answers include a destination when the next step exists. Story questions still
use the shared Narrative Intelligence context and cite the affected story
elements.
