## Authority and safety

Every packet contained seven of seven unique confirmed-planning records. No author decision, canon entry, or confirmed-planning entry disappeared. Candidate, pending, rejected, stale, superseded, archived, and inactive rows were excluded from role projections.

Native retrieval and chapter intelligence can contain derived evidence that conflicts with authority, such as the stored two-year/five-year Elias discrepancy. The packet explicitly labels those channels as derived evidence and instructs the specialist to identify the conflict and follow the authority snapshot. The real outputs retained the five-year authority and treated conflicting evidence as a discrepancy rather than replacing authority.

No chronology or character-knowledge safety regression was detected by the P0 suites. Memory P0, Phase A, and provider-boundary focused tests all passed.

## Cross-agent coherence

The real optimized workflow completed all five roles. Structured handoffs were visible to every downstream role. All outputs preserved the shared Chapter 10 reveal boundary, and the later Arc/Pacing agents incorporated earlier relationship, journey, and mystery direction.

## Blind/matched interpretation

The legacy input replay used the exact real optimized outputs, so packet cost changed while output content did not. This proves the 91.54% input reduction does not require deleting specialist output. It does not prove that a live legacy provider would have produced identical quality; the live legacy run failed before completing the roster.

## Quality limitation

The checks are deterministic requirement coverage, authority coverage, safety filtering, and cross-role consistency—not a full literary preference study. A future evaluation may add blinded human scoring across several genres, but Phase E does not require a new evaluation agent or provider dependency.
